# Validation Outreach Kit

Concrete, sendable material for the 90-day commercial-validation window (`00-business/pharos-current-strategy.md`). Everything here is an AI-assisted first draft; per `CLAUDE.md`, Jason Hill reviews and approves before anything goes to a real prospect or partner, and none of it should go out before the business-readiness gate (question 5 in `09-project-management/decisions-required.md`) is closed for whatever it's actually being used for (see the note on each piece below).

Track every use of this material in `10_Admin/commercial-validation-tracker.csv`.

## 1. Buyer introduction (one short explanation of Pharos)

Use this in a LinkedIn message, an email, or as a spoken 20-second answer to "so what do you do?"

> I help New Zealand businesses answer a question almost nobody has independently checked: your IT provider manages your technology, but has anyone independently reviewed whether your security is actually good enough? I run a plain-English review, tell you what genuinely matters, and work with your existing IT provider on what to fix first. No products to sell, no jargon.

## 2. Warm introduction request (for former colleagues and personal contacts)

Can go out now; it's an introduction request, not a sales pitch, and doesn't require the legal/insurance gate to be closed (no work or payment is being proposed).

> Subject: A favour, not a pitch
>
> Hi [name],
>
> I've started Pharos Security, an independent cyber security review for New Zealand small and medium businesses; think of it as an outside check on whether the security around a business is actually appropriate, separate from whoever manages their IT day to day.
>
> I'm not asking you to buy anything. I'm looking for introductions to people who might genuinely benefit from a conversation: business owners, GMs, or finance leads at professional-services businesses (accounting, legal, engineering, property, that kind of thing), roughly 15 to 50 staff, who use an outsourced IT provider and don't have anyone in-house checking security specifically.
>
> If anyone comes to mind, I'd genuinely appreciate an introduction, even just by email. No pressure either way, and thanks for reading this far.
>
> [Jason]

## 3. MSP partner pitch (most important piece — read the framing note first)

**Framing note:** never open with anything that reads as "checking up on you" or "catching you out." The entire point is that Pharos becomes a source of well-scoped, pre-qualified project work for the MSP, and a way to have the security conversation independently when a client asks a question the MSP would rather not answer alone (conflict of interest, or simply outside their remit). Lead with what's in it for them, not what Pharos checks.

> Subject: An independent security review that sends you work, not scrutiny
>
> Hi [name],
>
> I run Pharos Security, an independent cyber security review for New Zealand SMEs. I'm reaching out to IT providers directly because the model only works alongside a good MSP, not instead of one.
>
> Here's the shape of it: I review a client's security from a business-risk perspective (identity, email, backups, external exposure, and so on), prioritise what actually matters, and hand over a clear, plain-English list of what to fix. Where I'm not the right fit, technical implementation stays entirely with you. I don't do managed IT, I don't sell software, and I have no interest in becoming your client's ongoing IT provider.
>
> What that can mean for you in practice:
>
> - **Independent backing for recommendations you've already made.** If you've told a client they need MFA everywhere or a backup review and they haven't acted, an independent third party saying the same thing often lands differently.
> - **A structured pipeline of scoped project work**, with priorities and a client-approved plan already in place, rather than an open-ended "can you also look at security" conversation.
> - **Someone else to have the harder security conversations**, the ones that are awkward for you to raise unprompted (a security gap that predates your engagement, a client who won't invest in something you've flagged).
> - **Less pressure to offer security consulting outside your comfort zone**, while still being the one who implements whatever's agreed.
>
> I'd genuinely value 15 minutes to see whether this is worth exploring, no commitment, and I'm equally happy to hear if it isn't a fit.
>
> [Jason]

## 4. Targeted, trigger-based outreach (short, specific, human)

Use the trigger that actually applies; never send a generic version of all of these at once.

**M365 security uncertainty**
> Quick one: do you know whether your Microsoft 365 security settings actually match what your business needs, or is that something you've assumed your IT provider has covered? I run an independent review that answers exactly that, happy to explain in 15 minutes if useful.

**Insurer security requirements**
> If your cyber insurance renewal has asked questions you weren't fully confident answering, that's a pretty common trigger for the review I run. Worth a quick conversation before your next renewal?

**Customer security questionnaire**
> Has a customer ever sent you a security questionnaire that took longer to answer than it should have, or that you weren't sure you'd answered well? That's a specific gap I help close, independently of your IT provider.

**BEC / invoice-fraud concern**
> A quick question: if someone sent a very convincing fake invoice tomorrow, are you confident your business would catch it? That's one of the specific things I check in the review I run, alongside the wider picture.

**Account compromise (near miss or actual)**
> I saw [reference to a relevant near-miss or general context, if genuinely known]. If your business has had a close call with a compromised account or suspicious login, that's usually the moment worth having an independent look, rather than assuming it was a one-off.

**Board/management assurance**
> If a board member or director has ever asked "are we doing enough on cyber security" and you didn't have a confident, evidence-based answer, that's exactly the gap this review closes.

**MSP security assurance**
> Genuine question: has anyone independently checked what your IT provider has in place for security, or is that entirely taken on trust? I do exactly that, working alongside your provider, not around them.

**Incident readiness**
> If something serious happened tomorrow, malware, a compromised account, a data exposure, would your team know exactly what to do in the first hour? That's usually not something anyone's written down until after it's needed.

## Usage rules

- One trigger per message. Don't stack multiple triggers into one generic pitch.
- Personalise the opening line every time; never send verbatim to a cold list.
- No manufactured urgency, no "act now," no discount-expires framing.
- Log every send in `10_Admin/commercial-validation-tracker.csv`, including the trigger used and the outcome, so the assumption register (`09-project-management/pharos-assumption-register.md`) has real data behind it, not impressions.
