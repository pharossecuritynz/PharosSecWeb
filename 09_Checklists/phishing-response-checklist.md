## How to spot a suspicious email

Watch for:
- **Urgency or pressure** — "act now," "your account will be closed," unusually urgent requests from "management"
- **Mismatched sender details** — the display name says one thing, but the actual email address looks different or slightly off
- **Unexpected requests** — especially anything involving payment, bank details, gift cards, or login credentials
- **Links that don't match** — hover over a link (without clicking) to see where it actually goes before trusting it
- **Something that feels slightly "off"** — trust that instinct. It's usually right.

## Before you click a link or open an attachment

Ask:
1. Was I expecting this email?
2. Does the sender's email address actually match who they claim to be?
3. Is this asking me to do something unusual — pay something, log in somewhere, share information?

If any answer raises doubt, **don't click** — verify first.

## If a request involves money or bank details

**Always verify by phone, using a number you already have on file** — never a number or contact detail provided in the email itself. This single habit stops the majority of invoice fraud.

## If you think you've clicked something you shouldn't have

1. **Tell someone immediately** — don't wait, don't worry about looking silly. The fastest response is the best defence.
2. If you entered a password anywhere suspicious, change that password immediately (and anywhere else you've reused it)
3. Disconnect the device from the network if instructed to
4. Follow your business's incident process (see your [Incident Readiness Plan](../05_Client_Templates/incident-readiness-plan-template.md) if one exists)

## Remember

**Reporting a mistake quickly is never the wrong move.** A blame-free culture around reporting is one of the most effective defences any business can have — nobody gets in trouble for flagging something, even if it turns out to be nothing.
