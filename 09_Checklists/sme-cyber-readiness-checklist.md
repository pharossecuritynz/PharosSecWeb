# SME Cyber Readiness Checklist

*A free, plain-English checklist for New Zealand small and medium businesses. Primary lead magnet — see [../06_Sales_and_Marketing/lead-magnet-ideas.md](../06_Sales_and_Marketing/lead-magnet-ideas.md). Designed to be genuinely useful standalone, not a teaser.*

**Pharos Security — Clear guidance. Safer business.**

Work through this list honestly — it's not a test, just a way to see where the biggest opportunities are. Tick what's already in place.

## Access and passwords

- [ ] Multi-factor authentication (MFA) — an extra code or app prompt beyond a password — is turned on for email and other key business systems
- [ ] Staff don't share logins or passwords
- [ ] There's a process for removing system access when someone leaves the business
- [ ] Passwords aren't reused across multiple business systems

## Email and phishing

- [ ] Staff know how to spot a suspicious email (unexpected requests, urgency, mismatched sender addresses)
- [ ] There's a rule that bank account or payment detail changes are always verified by phone, using a number you already have on file
- [ ] Staff know who to tell if they think they've clicked something they shouldn't have — and know it's safe to report without blame

## Devices

- [ ] Work devices (laptops, phones) have up-to-date software and security patches
- [ ] Devices are protected with a screen lock/passcode
- [ ] Lost or stolen devices can be remotely locked or wiped

## Backups

- [ ] Critical business data is backed up regularly
- [ ] Backups are stored somewhere separate from your main systems (so a single incident can't take out both)
- [ ] You've actually tested restoring from a backup at least once — not just assumed it works

## People and habits

- [ ] Staff have had some form of cyber security awareness training in the past 12 months
- [ ] There's a basic written policy (even a short one) covering acceptable use of business systems
- [ ] New staff get a basic security briefing as part of onboarding

## Incident readiness

- [ ] There's a written plan (even a simple one) for what to do if something goes wrong
- [ ] Key contacts (IT provider, bank, insurer) are documented somewhere accessible — not just in one person's head
- [ ] You know what CERT NZ is and how to report an incident (cert.govt.nz)

## Third parties and vendors

- [ ] You know which suppliers/vendors have access to your systems or data
- [ ] You've asked key vendors basic questions about how they protect your data

## What your results mean

**Mostly ticked?** You're in a good position — a periodic check-in (like an annual mini review) is likely all you need.

**A mix of ticked and unticked?** You've got a foundation, with some clear, achievable gaps worth prioritising. This is exactly the situation a [Cyber Risk Review](../03_Services/cyber-risk-review.md) is built for.

**Mostly unticked?** That's completely normal — most SMEs are in this position, and the good news is that the highest-impact fixes are usually straightforward. A conversation is a good next step, with zero pressure.
