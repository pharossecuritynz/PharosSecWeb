# Tagline Options

## Primary tagline

**Clear guidance. Safer business.**

Use this as the default tagline across the website, email signature, LinkedIn, and printed materials. It captures both halves of the value proposition (clarity + risk reduction) in four words, and pairs well with the beacon/guidance concept.

## Alternative taglines

| Tagline | Best used when... |
|---|---|
| Practical cyber security for New Zealand businesses. | A more literal, SEO-friendly line — useful in meta descriptions, LinkedIn headline, or anywhere the primary tagline's metaphor needs unpacking for a first-time reader. |
| Cyber security made clear for SMEs. | Good for ad copy or contexts introducing the brand to a cold audience who need the category spelled out immediately. |
| Stronger security starts with clear direction. | Works well as a section intro line or email subject line rather than a standalone tagline. |
| Guiding New Zealand businesses through cyber risk. | Useful in more formal/written contexts (proposals, partner one-pagers) where the "guiding" verb reinforces the advisory positioning. |
| Clarity, protection, confidence. | A three-word rhythm option for tight spaces (business card, social avatar bio) where a full sentence doesn't fit. |

## Usage guidance

- **Website hero:** use the full headline + subheading (see [../06_Sales_and_Marketing/website-copy.md](../06_Sales_and_Marketing/website-copy.md)) rather than the tagline alone — the tagline supports, it doesn't replace, the primary message.
- **Email signature:** "Clear guidance. Safer business." under the founder's name and title.
- **LinkedIn headline:** consider "Practical cyber security for New Zealand businesses." — more discoverable in search than the primary tagline.
- **Business card / letterhead:** primary tagline, small, under the wordmark.

## What to avoid in future tagline exploration

- Anything implying guaranteed protection ("Unbreakable," "100% secure")
- Anything fear-based ("Before it's too late," "Don't be the next headline")
- Anything overly technical or acronym-laden
- Anything that could apply to any consultancy in any industry (must feel specific to cyber security + NZ SMEs)
