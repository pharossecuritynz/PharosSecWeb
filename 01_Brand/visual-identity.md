# Visual Identity

## Concept

The visual identity is built around the **Pharos beacon** — an abstract representation of a lighthouse light: a fixed point, a guiding beam, clarity cutting through uncertainty. It is expressed abstractly (geometric marks, light beams, radiating lines) rather than literally (no lighthouse buildings, no coastline illustrations, no maritime clichés).

The overall feel is **premium boutique consultancy**: closer to a design studio or specialist advisory firm than a security vendor.

## Design principles

1. **Deep navy as the anchor.** Navy sections carry the brand's calm authority — used for the header, hero, process section, and final CTA.
2. **White and soft mist for content.** Detailed, information-dense sections (services, credibility) sit on white or soft mist backgrounds for readability and a premium, uncluttered feel.
3. **Teal/cyan used sparingly.** Accent colour is restrained — small UI elements, icons, borders, and the beacon glow. It should never dominate a layout.
4. **Generous whitespace.** Boutique, premium brands breathe. Avoid dense, cluttered layouts; err on the side of more space, not less.
5. **Abstract light, never literal danger.** Radiating lines, soft glows, and directional beams represent guidance and clarity — never targets, crosshairs, alerts, or warning iconography.
6. **Geometric, restrained iconography.** Line icons (1.5px stroke, rounded caps) in a consistent style — compass, map, checklist, mail, people, lifebuoy motifs rather than padlocks or shields-with-skulls.

## What to avoid

Explicitly avoid:
- Hooded figures, "hacker in a hoodie" imagery
- Matrix-style falling code, green terminal text
- Skulls, crosshairs, or weapon iconography
- Aggressive red/black fear-based colour schemes
- Padlock clichés as a primary visual device
- Military/tactical styling (camo, stencil fonts, combat imagery)
- Generic stock photography of people in server rooms wearing masks

## Imagery style (photography, when used)

Prefer: natural light, New Zealand business settings (offices, small teams, coastal or landscape backgrounds used sparingly and abstractly), genuine-feeling photography over overly staged corporate stock. Avoid literal "cyber security" stock imagery (glowing padlocks, binary code overlays, blue matrix grids).

## Layout patterns used on the website

- Alternating navy / white / mist sections to create rhythm down a long one-page layout
- Card grids with restrained borders and a subtle lift-on-hover interaction for services and value props
- A thin horizontal "beam" line connecting steps in the process section
- Radial glow elements (teal, heavily blurred) used behind hero and CTA headlines for warmth without noise

## Motion / interaction principles

- Motion should be subtle and slow — nothing sharp, blinking, or urgent
- Hover states: gentle lift + border colour shift, not scale/bounce effects
- Any ambient animation (e.g. the hero's glow) should be slow (6–10 second cycles) and low-opacity
