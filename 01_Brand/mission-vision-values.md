# Mission, Vision, Values

## Mission

To help New Zealand SMEs understand their cyber risk and take practical, proportionate action to reduce it — without jargon, without fear, and without paying for enterprise-grade complexity they don't need.

## Vision

A New Zealand where small and medium businesses treat cyber security as a normal, manageable part of running a business — not a mystery they've outsourced entirely, ignored out of overwhelm, or over-bought out of fear.

## Promise to clients

We will always:
- Explain things in plain English first, technical detail second
- Recommend only what's proportionate to your size, risk, and budget
- Tell you when something isn't worth doing yet
- Work alongside your existing IT provider, not around them
- Stay independent — no product resale margins, no vendor kickbacks shaping advice

## Values

| Value | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| **Clarity** | Every report, conversation, and recommendation should be understandable to a business owner with no security background. If a client can't repeat back what we told them, we haven't done our job. |
| **Proportion** | Advice is sized to the client's actual risk and resources. We don't recommend enterprise controls to a 12-person business, and we don't understate real risk to make a sale easier. |
| **Independence** | We don't sell security products, take referral commissions from vendors, or let a tooling relationship bias a recommendation. If we suggest a tool, it's because it's genuinely the right fit. |
| **Calm over fear** | We never use scare tactics, breach statistics as pressure, or manufactured urgency to close a sale. Clients should leave a conversation with clarity and confidence, not anxiety. |
| **Practicality** | Recommendations must be achievable by a real SME with limited time, budget, and technical staff. A brilliant control nobody can maintain is a bad recommendation. |
| **Honesty about limits** | We are clear about what this business does and doesn't do (see [service-boundaries.md](../03_Services/service-boundaries.md)). When something is outside scope — active incident response, penetration testing, legal advice — we say so and refer out. |

## Operating principles

1. **Plain English first.** Every deliverable leads with what it means for the business, not the technical mechanism.
2. **Right-sized, always.** Every recommendation is checked against: "Could this business realistically do this in the next 90 days?"
3. **No scare tactics.** We don't open conversations with breach statistics or worst-case scenarios designed to create urgency.
4. **Work with the IT provider, not around them.** Most clients already have an MSP or IT contractor. We position ourselves as complementary — the independent voice on risk and priority, not a replacement for day-to-day IT support.
5. **Document everything simply.** Reports and plans should be usable by a non-technical owner without a follow-up call to interpret them.
6. **Say no to bad-fit work.** If a prospective engagement genuinely needs penetration testing, forensic incident response, or compliance certification support we can't credibly provide, we refer out rather than stretch scope.
7. **Protect client information as if it were our own.** See [../04_Operating_Manual/data-handling-and-confidentiality.md](../04_Operating_Manual/data-handling-and-confidentiality.md).

## Assumptions / needs founder input

- These values assume a solo founder-led practice. If Pharos Security later takes on associates or subcontractors, values should be revisited to include team-facing principles (e.g. how associates represent the brand).
- "Vision" is intentionally broad; the founder may want to sharpen it once the first 12–24 months of client work reveals a narrower niche (e.g. a particular industry vertical).
