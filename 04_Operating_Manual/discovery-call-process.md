# Discovery Call Process (SOP)

The free, low-pressure conversation that qualifies fit and identifies the right entry-point service. 15 minutes, structured. This is not a free assessment: it ends with a fit decision and a clear next step, not technical conclusions or remediation advice. See [../00-business/decisions.md](../00-business/decisions.md) (2026-07-22) for why 15 minutes was confirmed over the previous 20 to 30 minute guidance.

## Purpose

- Understand the business and its real cyber risk context
- Identify what's actually worrying the client, which may differ from what they first asked about
- Recommend the right-fit service (see the selection guide in [../03_Services/service-catalogue.md](../03_Services/service-catalogue.md)), or say plainly that Pharos is not the right fit
- Set realistic expectations on price and timeline
- Decide, mutually, whether to proceed to a proposal

## Structure

### Minute 0 to 2: introduction and purpose
- Thank them for their time, confirm the call is free and no-obligation
- Set the frame: "This is 15 minutes to understand your situation and work out honestly whether, and how, Pharos can help, including if the answer is that you don't need anything from me right now."

### Minute 2 to 6: current situation and concern
Ask open questions, listen more than talk:
- What prompted them to reach out now? This is the single most useful question; the real trigger often reveals the real need.
- What's their biggest current worry, in their own words?
- Have they had any specific incident or near miss?

### Minute 6 to 10: environment and capability
- What does the business do, roughly how many staff, what systems do they rely on day to day?
- Do they have an IT provider or MSP? What's that relationship like?
- What, if anything, have they already done about cyber security?

### Minute 10 to 13: priorities and fit
- Summarise what was heard back to them in plain English (builds trust, confirms understanding)
- Recommend the right entry-point service, or say plainly if the answer is "you probably don't need to spend money on this yet" or "your IT provider likely already has this covered, ask them X"
- If the request is for something Pharos does not do (see [../03_Services/service-boundaries.md](../03_Services/service-boundaries.md)), say so directly and, where a referral exists, offer it
- Explain roughly what the recommended service involves and the price range; the exact quote comes in the proposal, never live on the call

### Minute 13 to 15: next step
- Confirm the specific next step: "I'll send a short proposal by [date] with the scope and price confirmed, no obligation to proceed," or a clear no-fit close, or a referral
- Ask if there's anyone else who should be included in that email, for example a business partner or their IT provider

## Risks to avoid

- Turning this into a free assessment: no technical conclusions, no diagnosing the organisation, no detailed remediation advice on the call itself
- Quoting an exact price live: give a range, confirm the figure in writing after reflecting on scope
- Pressure-closing: no "this offer expires," no artificial urgency
- Running over 15 minutes as a habit: if it happens consistently, that is a signal to review the structure, not to quietly let every call run long

## Service fit indicators

Recommend proceeding when the business is a New Zealand SME (roughly 5 to 100 staff per [../02_Business_Strategy/target-market.md](../02_Business_Strategy/target-market.md)), has no in-house security specialist, and the concern raised maps to something in [../03_Services/service-catalogue.md](../03_Services/service-catalogue.md). Recommend a no-fit or referral close when the business needs active technical testing (currently out of scope, see [../00-business/decisions.md](../00-business/decisions.md) 2026-07-22), is materially outside the target size range, or is describing an active incident in progress (see [escalation-and-referral-rules.md](escalation-and-referral-rules.md)).

## Rules for this call

- **Never quote an exact price live on the call.** Give a range, confirm the exact figure in writing after reflecting on scope.
- **Never pressure-close.** No artificial urgency.
- **It's okay to end the call recommending nothing.** This builds more long-term trust and referral value than pushing a sale that isn't a fit.
- **Take notes during, not after.** Memory fades fast and accurate notes make the proposal faster to write.

```
Business name:
Contact name / role:
Staff count (approx):

Trigger for reaching out:

Other attendees to include:
```

## No-fit wording

Direct and warm, not apologetic: "Based on what you've described, I don't think [service] is the right starting point for you, and I'd rather say that now than take your money for something that isn't the best use of it." Offer a referral where one exists (see [escalation-and-referral-rules.md](escalation-and-referral-rules.md)).

## Follow-up checklist

1. Log notes in the CRM/tracker within the same day.
2. Send the proposal within the timeframe promised (see [proposal-process.md](proposal-process.md)). Never let this slip; it is the single biggest trust-builder or trust-breaker in the sales process.
3. If the outcome was "not a fit right now," send a brief, warm follow-up anyway. Many of these become clients later.

## Assumptions / needs founder input

- None. This process is ready to use as-is. Refine the question set after the first 5 to 10 real calls based on what actually surfaces useful information, and confirm 15 minutes is genuinely enough time once tested against real calls.
