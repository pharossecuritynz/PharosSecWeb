# Discovery Call Process (SOP)

The free, low-pressure conversation that qualifies fit and identifies the right entry-point service. Typically 20–30 minutes.

## Purpose

- Understand the business and its real cyber risk context
- Identify what's actually worrying the client (which may differ from what they first asked about)
- Recommend the right-fit service (see the selection guide in [../03_Services/service-catalogue.md](../03_Services/service-catalogue.md))
- Set realistic expectations on price and timeline
- Decide, mutually, whether to proceed to a proposal

## Structure

### Opening (2–3 minutes)
- Thank them for their time, confirm the call is free and no-obligation
- Set the frame: "I'll ask a few questions to understand your business, then tell you honestly what I think would help — including if I think you don't need anything from me right now."

### Understand the business (8–10 minutes)
Ask open questions, listen more than talk:
- What does the business do, roughly how many staff, what systems do they rely on day to day?
- Do they have an IT provider/MSP? What's that relationship like?
- What prompted them to reach out now? (This is the single most useful question — the real trigger often reveals the real need.)
- What (if anything) have they already done about cyber security?
- What's their biggest current worry, in their own words?

### Reflect and recommend (5–8 minutes)
- Summarise what you heard back to them in plain English (builds trust, confirms understanding)
- Recommend the right entry-point service — be honest if the answer is "you probably don't need to spend money on this yet" or "your IT provider likely already has this covered, ask them X"
- Explain roughly what the recommended service involves and the price range (exact quote comes in the proposal)

### Close (2–3 minutes)
- Confirm next steps: "I'll send a short proposal by [date] with the scope and price confirmed — no obligation to proceed."
- Ask if there's anyone else who should be included in that email (e.g. a business partner, their IT provider)

## Rules for this call

- **Never quote an exact price live on the call** — give a range, confirm the exact figure in writing after reflecting on scope
- **Never pressure-close.** No "this offer expires," no artificial urgency
- **It's okay to end the call recommending nothing** — this builds more long-term trust and referral value than pushing a sale that isn't a fit
- **Take notes during, not after** — memory fades fast and accurate notes make the proposal faster to write

## Call notes template

```
Business name:
Contact name / role:
Staff count (approx):
Current IT provider (if any):
Trigger for reaching out:
Current state (what they've already done):
Biggest stated concern:
Recommended service:
Price range discussed:
Next step / date proposal is due:
Other attendees to include:
```

## After the call

1. Log notes in the CRM/tracker within the same day
2. Send the proposal within the timeframe promised (see [proposal-process.md](proposal-process.md)) — **never let this slip**, it's the single biggest trust-builder or trust-breaker in the sales process
3. If the outcome was "not a fit right now," send a brief, warm follow-up anyway — many of these become clients later

## Assumptions / needs founder input

- None — this process is ready to use as-is. Refine the question set after the first 5–10 real calls based on what actually surfaces useful information.
