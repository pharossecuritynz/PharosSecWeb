# Delivery Workflow (SOP)

The general pattern every engagement follows once a proposal is accepted. Service-specific steps live in each file under [../03_Services/](../03_Services/); this document covers the common shape.

## Standard engagement lifecycle

```
Proposal accepted
   │
   ▼
1. Kickoff — confirm logistics, collect inputs
   │
   ▼
2. Data/information gathering — questionnaire, interviews, access
   │
   ▼
3. Analysis and drafting
   │
   ▼
4. Quality check (see quality-checklist.md)
   │
   ▼
5. Draft/findings review with client
   │
   ▼
6. Finalisation and delivery
   │
   ▼
7. Close-out — feedback, invoice, next-step conversation
```

## 1. Kickoff

- Send a kickoff email confirming: timeline, what's needed from the client and by when, who the point of contact is
- Send the relevant intake document (e.g. [discovery-questionnaire.md](../05_Client_Templates/discovery-questionnaire.md))
- Schedule any required sessions (interviews, workshops, access windows)

## 2. Data/information gathering

- Follow the specific process for the service (interview, technical access review, workshop)
- If the client is slow to provide inputs, follow up proactively — don't let the engagement stall silently. Two follow-ups over 5 business days is reasonable before escalating with a friendly check-in call.

## 3. Analysis and drafting

- Use the relevant template from [../05_Client_Templates/](../05_Client_Templates/)
- Write the plain-English summary **last**, after the detailed findings are drafted — it's easier to summarise accurately once the full picture is clear
- Flag any findings that raise questions about scope boundaries (e.g. something that looks like it needs pentesting or legal input) rather than guessing

## 4. Quality check

- Every deliverable goes through [quality-checklist.md](quality-checklist.md) before being shared in draft or final form — no exceptions, even under time pressure

## 5. Draft/findings review with client

- For larger engagements (Risk Review, Uplift Plan), share findings in a live call rather than just emailing a document cold — this catches misunderstandings early and is a better client experience
- Note any requested changes and apply them before finalising

## 6. Finalisation and delivery

- Deliver in the agreed format (PDF for reports, Word if the client wants an editable version)
- Send a short cover email summarising what's attached and any immediate next step

## 7. Close-out

- Send the final invoice (if not already paid via deposit/balance structure)
- Ask for feedback (see [../04_Operating_Manual/client-communication-standards.md](client-communication-standards.md)) and, if appropriate, a testimonial
- Note any natural follow-on service to mention (e.g. after a Risk Review, mention the Uplift Plan) — but only if genuinely relevant, never as a reflexive upsell
- Update the CRM/tracker: mark complete, log actual time spent (for future estimating accuracy), log any lessons learned

## Timeline expectations by service (typical, from kickoff to delivery)

| Service | Typical elapsed time |
|---|---|
| Cyber Risk Review | 1.5–2.5 weeks |
| SME Security Uplift Plan | 1.5–2.5 weeks |
| Microsoft 365 Basics Review | 1–1.5 weeks |
| Email and Phishing Readiness | 1–1.5 weeks |
| Incident Readiness Workshop | 1–2 weeks (mostly scheduling-dependent) |
| Staff Cyber Awareness Session | 1–2 weeks (mostly scheduling-dependent) |
| Security Policy Starter Pack | 1–2 weeks |

## Assumptions / needs founder input

- Timelines assume a solo founder working other active engagements concurrently; tighten or loosen these once real throughput data exists.
