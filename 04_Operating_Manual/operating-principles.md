# Operating Principles

The rules that govern how Pharos Security actually runs, day to day — the practical companion to [../01_Brand/mission-vision-values.md](../01_Brand/mission-vision-values.md).

## 1. Every engagement starts with a free discovery call

No proposal is sent, and no price is quoted, without a live conversation first. This protects both sides from mismatched expectations and lets the founder qualify fit before committing time. See [discovery-call-process.md](discovery-call-process.md).

## 2. Scope is written down before work starts

Every paid engagement has a written proposal/Statement of Work confirming scope, deliverables, timeline, and price before any billable work begins. See [proposal-process.md](proposal-process.md).

## 3. Reports are written for the owner, reviewed for the technical reader

Every client-facing document leads with a plain-English executive summary, then technical detail. See [reporting-standards.md](reporting-standards.md).

## 4. Nothing goes to a client without a quality check

Every deliverable passes through the [quality-checklist.md](quality-checklist.md) before sending — even under time pressure. A rushed, error-filled report does more brand damage than a delayed one.

## 5. Confidentiality is absolute

Client information, findings, and even the fact of an engagement are never disclosed without explicit permission. See [data-handling-and-confidentiality.md](data-handling-and-confidentiality.md).

## 6. Out-of-scope work is refused or referred, not stretched

If a request falls outside [../03_Services/service-boundaries.md](../03_Services/service-boundaries.md), say so and refer out. Scope creep to avoid an awkward conversation is a bigger risk than losing the fee.

## 7. Capacity is protected deliberately

The founder is the only delivery resource. Retainer clients, project work, and business development time must be actively balanced — see [weekly-business-rhythm.md](weekly-business-rhythm.md). Overcommitting degrades quality for everyone.

## 8. Every client interaction reflects the brand voice

Calm, plain-English, no jargon, no fear tactics — in emails, calls, and reports alike. See [../01_Brand/tone-of-voice.md](../01_Brand/tone-of-voice.md) and [client-communication-standards.md](client-communication-standards.md).

## 9. Feedback loops are built in, not optional

Every completed engagement includes a request for feedback and (where appropriate) a testimonial. This isn't just business development — it's how the service catalogue and pricing get sharper over time.

## 10. When in doubt, choose clarity over cleverness

If a decision — in wording, scope, or pricing — comes down to "impressive-sounding" vs. "genuinely clear," choose clear.
