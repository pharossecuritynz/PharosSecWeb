# Escalation and Referral Rules

Clear rules for when to say "that's not something I do" — and what to do instead.

## Category A: Urgent / possible live incident

**Signal:** A client or prospect contacts Pharos Security saying (in effect) "something is happening right now" — ransomware, suspected breach, fraudulent payment in progress, systems locked/inaccessible.

**Action — immediately, same day:**
1. Respond immediately, calmly. Do not treat as a normal enquiry or wait for the standard response window.
2. Provide immediate, plain-English first-step guidance **only** where confident to do so safely (e.g. "disconnect the affected device from the network," "contact your bank immediately if a payment was involved," "don't pay a ransom or engage with attackers directly").
3. Direct them to the appropriate emergency resources:
   - **NCSC** — ncsc.govt.nz/report or 0800 114 115 (report cyber security incidents; CERT NZ has fully merged into the NCSC)
   - **NZ Police** — 105 (non-emergency) or 111 (if there's immediate danger or large-scale fraud in progress)
   - **Their bank**, immediately, if a fraudulent payment is involved
   - **Their cyber insurer** (if they have a policy) — many policies require using an approved incident response panel
4. Be explicit that Pharos Security does not provide live technical incident response, and help them understand what kind of help to look for (a forensic IR firm, their insurer's panel).
5. Follow up afterwards (once the immediate situation is resolved) to see if Incident Readiness or an Independent Security Review would help prevent a repeat — but never during the crisis itself.

## Category B: Out-of-scope service request

**Signal:** A prospect or client asks for penetration testing, 24/7 monitoring, compliance certification, or another explicitly out-of-scope service (see [../03_Services/service-boundaries.md](../03_Services/service-boundaries.md)).

**Action:**
1. Acknowledge the request positively — don't make them feel silly for asking
2. Explain plainly that it's outside what Pharos Security provides, and briefly why (protects them, not just a policy)
3. Offer a referral if a trusted partner exists (see [../02_Business_Strategy/competitor-positioning.md](../02_Business_Strategy/competitor-positioning.md) for partner categories)
4. Where possible, offer an adjacent way to still help (e.g. "I can't run the pentest, but I'm happy to help you scope what you need and interpret the results afterwards")

## Category C: Scope creep within an active engagement

**Signal:** A client asks for "just one more thing" that's reasonable-sounding but outside the agreed proposal.

**Action:**
1. If small and low-effort (under ~30 minutes), it's fine to use judgement and just help — goodwill matters
2. If more than trivial, say so plainly: "Happy to help with that — it's outside what we scoped, so let me send a quick add-on quote" — don't silently absorb meaningful extra work
3. Log the pattern — if this happens often with a particular service, the base scope may need adjusting (see [../03_Services/service-catalogue.md](../03_Services/service-catalogue.md))

## Category D: Referral partner network

Build and maintain relationships with:

| Need | Refer to |
|---|---|
| Penetration testing | A licensed NZ pentest firm |
| Forensic incident response | A digital forensics/IR firm (ideally one on the client's cyber insurer's approved panel, if they have one) |
| Legal advice (breach notification, contracts) | A commercial/privacy lawyer |
| Compliance certification (ISO 27001, SOC 2) | An accredited certification body / compliance consultancy |
| Day-to-day IT implementation | The client's existing MSP, or a recommended MSP if they don't have one |

See [../06_Sales_and_Marketing/referral-partner-one-pager.md](../06_Sales_and_Marketing/referral-partner-one-pager.md) for the outreach material used to build these relationships.
