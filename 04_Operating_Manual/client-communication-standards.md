# Client Communication Standards

How Pharos Security communicates with clients and prospects, across every channel.

## Response time standards

| Channel | Standard response time |
|---|---|
| New enquiry (website/email/LinkedIn) | Within 1 business day |
| Active engagement — client email | Within 1 business day |
| Retainer client — Standard/Priority tier | Within 1 business day (Priority), 2 business days (Standard) |
| Retainer client — Essentials tier | Within 2 business days |
| Urgent/incident-adjacent message | Same day — see [escalation-and-referral-rules.md](escalation-and-referral-rules.md) |

If a fuller response will take longer than the standard, send a brief acknowledgement within the standard time and commit to a specific follow-up date.

## Channel guidance

- **Email** — primary channel for anything requiring a record (proposals, findings, confirmations)
- **Phone/video call** — for discovery calls, findings walkthroughs, workshops, and anything nuanced or sensitive
- **SMS/text** — acceptable for logistics only (confirming a call time), never for substantive advice or findings
- **LinkedIn messages** — fine for initial contact, but move to email once a real conversation starts

## Tone rules (apply [../01_Brand/tone-of-voice.md](../01_Brand/tone-of-voice.md) to every message)

- Every email opens with a warm, human line — not a template-feeling greeting
- No corporate filler ("As per my last email," "Please find attached")
- Say "I" not "we" unless genuinely referring to a team (keeps communication personal and honest about being a founder-led practice)
- When delivering bad news (a finding, a delay, a price increase) lead with the fact plainly, then the context — don't bury it

## Setting expectations early and often

- State timelines in every kickoff email, and proactively flag if something will run late — don't wait to be asked
- Restate scope boundaries plainly whenever a client asks for something adjacent to but outside the agreed scope (see [escalation-and-referral-rules.md](escalation-and-referral-rules.md))

## Handling difficult conversations

| Situation | Approach |
|---|---|
| Client pushes back on price | Explain what drives the price; offer to adjust scope rather than just discounting |
| Client is slow providing inputs | Friendly proactive check-in, not a passive-aggressive reminder |
| Client is unhappy with findings ("that can't be right") | Listen fully, walk through the evidence calmly, be open to being wrong if genuinely mistaken — but don't soften accurate findings just to avoid discomfort |
| Client asks for out-of-scope work mid-engagement | Acknowledge the request positively, explain it's outside current scope, offer to quote it separately |
| Client wants to cancel | Be gracious, ask (once, genuinely) what could have made it work, don't chase aggressively |

## Feedback requests

At the close of every engagement, send a short, specific feedback request (not just "how did we do?"):
- What was most useful?
- What would you change?
- Would you be comfortable being a reference or providing a short testimonial?
