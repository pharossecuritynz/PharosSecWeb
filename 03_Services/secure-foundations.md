# Secure Foundations

**Renamed and reframed from SME Security Uplift Plan, 2026-08-22, under the Pharos v2 directive.** Reframed as a bounded coordination period, not an open-ended plan document, to make clear Pharos does not become the client's MSP. Previous version archived at `archive/sme-security-uplift-plan.md`. See `00-business/decisions.md`.

## Purpose

A structured period in which Pharos helps a client and their IT provider work through the highest-priority recommendations from an Independent Security Review, IT Provider Security Assurance, or Exposure Snapshot.

## Who it's for

Businesses that have a reasonable picture of their cyber risk and are ready to act, but need help sequencing, coordinating, and verifying the work, especially where budget and internal resourcing are limited.

## Problems it solves

- "We know our gaps, but not how to sequence fixing them."
- "We need a plan we can actually resource and follow, not a 40-item checklist."
- "We need something to hand our IT provider that tells them exactly what to prioritise and why, and someone to check it actually got done."
- "We need to show leadership or the board a credible improvement roadmap."

## What Pharos does during Secure Foundations

Pharos coordinates, prioritises, reviews, verifies, advises, tracks, retests, and documents. The client's IT provider or internal team performs the technical implementation. Some lightweight Pharos work may be possible where clearly within scope and competency, but advisory independence stays the primary model: Pharos does not become the client's MSP.

## Scope

- Review of existing findings (from a prior Independent Security Review, IT Provider Security Assurance, or Exposure Snapshot, or a condensed assessment if starting fresh)
- Prioritisation using likelihood, business impact, cost, difficulty, dependencies, and available capability, not technical severity alone
- A 90-Day Security Improvement Plan (Now / Next / Later, or First 30 / 31 to 60 / 61 to 90 days)
- Clear ownership guidance: what the business does internally, what goes to the IT provider, and what needs a specialist referral
- Periodic check-ins to verify progress and retest completed items
- Available add-ons within this engagement: a staff awareness session, or a policy and checklist set (see `archive/staff-cyber-awareness-sessions.md`, `archive/security-policy-checklist-starter-pack.md` for source scope and pricing)

## Out of scope

- Hands-on implementation of the plan (specified by Pharos; implemented by the client's IT provider or internal team, or picked up ongoing under Security Adviser)
- Vendor selection or procurement management beyond high-level advice

## Deliverables

1. Planning session (60 to 90 minutes) to confirm priorities, constraints, and resourcing reality
2. 90-Day Security Improvement Plan document (see `05_Client_Templates/security-uplift-plan-template.md`) with a phased roadmap, ownership notes, and relative cost/effort per item
3. A handover call, ideally including the client's IT provider, to align on execution
4. Verification check-ins across the period to confirm what's actually been completed, not just planned

## Inputs required from client

- Prior review findings, or willingness to complete a condensed assessment first
- A realistic view of internal capacity and budget appetite
- IT provider contact details, if the client wants them included in the handover call

## Delivery process

1. Scoping and proposal
2. Planning session
3. Roadmap drafting (allow 3 to 5 business days)
4. Draft review with client, adjust based on feedback
5. Handover call, with the IT provider if applicable
6. Final plan delivered, followed by periodic verification check-ins across the agreed period

## Estimated effort (founder time)

| Task | Time |
|---|---|
| Scoping/proposal | 1 hour |
| Planning session | 1.5 hours |
| Roadmap drafting | 5–8 hours |
| Draft review & revision | 1.5 hours |
| Handover call | 1 hour |
| **Total (excl. check-ins)** | **~10–13 hours** |

## Suggested pricing range

**NZD $2,000 – $5,000** (excl. GST), depending on complexity and whether a fresh assessment is needed. See `02_Business_Strategy/pricing-strategy.md`.

## Risks and limitations

- Plan quality depends on realistic client input about budget and capacity; an overly ambitious plan the client can't resource will fail regardless of how well it's written
- Execution risk sits with the client and their IT provider, not Pharos, unless Security Adviser is also engaged
- Should be revisited if the business changes materially (new systems, growth, acquisition) within the plan period

## Sales copy

> Knowing your risks is only half the job. Secure Foundations turns findings into a realistic, achievable plan, coordinates the work with your IT provider, and checks it actually gets done, without Pharos becoming your IT provider.
