# Incident Readiness Workshop

## Purpose

A guided, half-day workshop that leaves a business with a clear, calm, written plan for what to do if something goes wrong — before it happens.

## Who it's for

Businesses that have no documented plan for handling a cyber incident (the vast majority of SMEs) and recognise that figuring it out *during* a crisis is far worse than preparing in advance.

## Problems it solves

- "We have no idea what we'd actually do if we got hit by ransomware or a breach."
- "We don't know who to call, in what order, or what we're legally required to do."
- "We want our team to have practiced this, even briefly, before it's real."

## Scope

- A facilitated workshop (half-day, in-person or remote) with key decision-makers (owner, ops lead, IT provider if possible)
- Walkthrough of realistic incident scenarios relevant to the business (e.g. ransomware, business email compromise, lost device, data breach involving customer data)
- Development of a one-page Incident Readiness Plan: key contacts, decision tree, communication approach, notification obligations (flagged for legal review, not provided as legal advice)
- A short "who do we call" reference card

## Out of scope

- Live incident response or technical remediation (see [../02_Business_Strategy/risk-boundaries.md](../02_Business_Strategy/risk-boundaries.md))
- Legal advice on breach notification obligations (the plan flags *that* legal input may be needed, and points to the Privacy Act 2020 and Office of the Privacy Commissioner guidance, but does not provide legal advice itself)
- Full disaster recovery / business continuity planning for non-cyber events (can be discussed as an extension if there's demand)

## Deliverables

1. Half-day facilitated workshop
2. Incident Readiness Plan document (see [../05_Client_Templates/incident-readiness-plan-template.md](../05_Client_Templates/incident-readiness-plan-template.md))
3. "Who to call" quick-reference card (see [../09_Checklists/incident-readiness-checklist.md](../09_Checklists/incident-readiness-checklist.md))

## Inputs required from client

- Availability of key decision-makers for the full workshop session
- Existing contact details for IT provider, insurer, bank, and (if applicable) legal counsel
- Any existing (even informal) notes on what they'd currently do in an incident

## Delivery process

1. Scoping/proposal, confirm attendees and format (in-person/remote)
2. Pre-workshop prep: tailor scenarios to the business's actual risk profile
3. Workshop delivery (half-day)
4. Plan drafting based on workshop outputs (allow 2–3 business days)
5. Draft review with client
6. Final plan and reference card delivered

## Risks and limitations

- A plan is only as good as its last review — recommend revisiting annually or after any significant business change (flag this in the plan itself)
- Effectiveness depends on the right decision-makers actually attending the workshop
- Must be explicit that this is *preparation*, not a substitute for professional incident response when a real event occurs

## Sales copy

> Nobody thinks clearly in the middle of a crisis. The Incident Readiness Workshop gets your key people in a room (or on a call) before anything's gone wrong, walks through what a real incident might look like for your business, and leaves you with a clear, calm plan — so if it ever happens, you already know what to do.
