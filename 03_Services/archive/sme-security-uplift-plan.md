# SME Security Uplift Plan

## Purpose

Turns identified risks (typically from a Cyber Risk Review) into a realistic, sequenced, resourced plan the business can actually execute over the following 6–12 months.

## Who it's for

Businesses that have completed (or already have a reasonable picture of) their cyber risk and are ready to act, but need help translating findings into a concrete, achievable plan — especially where budget and internal resourcing are limited.

## Problems it solves

- "We know our gaps, but not how to sequence fixing them."
- "We need a plan we can actually resource and follow — not a 40-item checklist."
- "We need something to hand to our IT provider that tells them exactly what to prioritise and why."
- "We need to show leadership/the board a credible improvement roadmap."

## Scope

- Review of existing risk findings (from a prior Risk Review, or a condensed assessment if starting fresh)
- Prioritisation using effort vs. impact, tailored to the client's actual budget and internal capability
- A phased roadmap (typically 3 phases across 6–12 months)
- Clear ownership guidance: what the business should do internally, what to hand to the IT provider, and what (if anything) needs external specialist help
- Success measures for each phase

## Out of scope

- Implementation of the plan itself (Pharos Security specifies *what* and *why*; implementation is done by the client's IT provider or internal team, optionally with ongoing advisory support via [Ongoing Cyber Advisor Support](ongoing-cyber-advisor-support.md))
- Vendor selection/procurement management (can advise at a high level, but not run a full procurement process)

## Deliverables

1. Planning session (60–90 minutes) to confirm priorities, constraints, and resourcing reality
2. SME Security Uplift Plan document (see [../05_Client_Templates/security-uplift-plan-template.md](../05_Client_Templates/security-uplift-plan-template.md)) including:
   - Phased roadmap (Now / Next 90 days / Next 6–12 months)
   - Ownership and resourcing notes per item
   - Estimated relative cost/effort per item (not exact vendor quotes)
3. A handover call, ideally including the client's IT provider, to align on execution

## Inputs required from client

- Prior Cyber Risk Review findings (or willingness to complete a condensed assessment first)
- Realistic view of internal capacity and budget appetite
- IT provider contact details, if the client wants them included in the handover call

## Delivery process

1. Scoping/proposal — confirm whether this follows an existing Risk Review or needs a condensed assessment first
2. Planning session
3. Roadmap drafting (allow 3–5 business days)
4. Draft review with client, adjust based on feedback
5. Handover call (with IT provider if applicable)
6. Final plan delivered

## Estimated effort (founder time)

| Task | Time |
|---|---|
| Scoping/proposal | 1 hour |
| Planning session | 1.5 hours |
| Roadmap drafting | 5–8 hours |
| Draft review & revision | 1.5 hours |
| Handover call | 1 hour |
| **Total** | **~10–13 hours** |

## Suggested pricing range

**NZD $2,000 – $5,000** (excl. GST), depending on complexity and whether a fresh assessment is needed. See [../02_Business_Strategy/pricing-strategy.md](../02_Business_Strategy/pricing-strategy.md).

## Risks and limitations

- Plan quality depends on realistic client input about budget/capacity — an overly ambitious plan the client can't resource will fail regardless of how well it's written
- Execution risk sits with the client/IT provider, not Pharos Security, unless Ongoing Cyber Advisor Support is also engaged
- Should be revisited if the business changes materially (new systems, growth, acquisition) within the plan period

## Sales copy

> Knowing your risks is only half the job — the other half is knowing what to actually do about them, in what order, with the budget and team you've got. The SME Security Uplift Plan turns a list of findings into a realistic roadmap you (and your IT provider) can actually follow.
