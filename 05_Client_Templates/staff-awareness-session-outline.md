# Staff Cyber Awareness Session — Facilitation Outline

*Structure for delivering the [Staff Cyber Awareness Sessions](../03_Services/staff-cyber-awareness-sessions.md) service. Target: 60–90 minutes, interactive, plain-English throughout.*

---

## Session goals

By the end, attendees should be able to: recognise a likely phishing attempt, know what to do if they think they've made a mistake, and understand the small handful of habits that matter most.

## Pre-session prep

- Confirm attendee count and format (in-person/remote)
- Gather 1–2 anonymised, realistic examples relevant to the client's industry (with permission if drawn from a real prior engagement — otherwise use generalised, plausible examples)
- Tailor terminology to the client's actual tools (e.g. reference Microsoft 365/Outlook if that's what they use)

## Outline

### 1. Opening (5 minutes)
- Introduce the session as practical and blame-free, not a compliance lecture
- Set the tone: "The goal today isn't to make you paranoid — it's to make a few habits automatic."

### 2. Why this matters, briefly (10 minutes)
- One or two real-world-style examples (no scare tactics, just realistic scenarios)
- Frame: most incidents start with something ordinary-looking — a "normal" email, a link, an invoice

### 3. Spotting phishing and social engineering (20 minutes)
- Walk through 3–4 realistic example emails/messages (a mix of legitimate and suspicious)
- Interactive: ask the group "would you click this?" before revealing the answer
- Key signals to look for: mismatched sender addresses, urgency/pressure language, unexpected requests (especially payment or credential-related), links that don't match the claimed destination

### 4. Passwords and MFA (10 minutes)
- Plain-English explanation of why MFA matters (a stolen password alone isn't enough to get in)
- Practical guidance: use a password manager if available, don't reuse passwords across work and personal accounts

### 5. Everyday habits (10 minutes)
- Safe use of personal devices for work
- Safe remote/public wifi practices
- Locking screens, secure disposal of sensitive documents

### 6. What to do if something goes wrong (10 minutes)
- Reinforce a **blame-free reporting culture**: "If you click something you shouldn't have, tell us immediately — the fastest response is the best defence, and nobody gets in trouble for reporting it."
- Walk through who to tell and how (reference the client's actual internal process, or the [Incident Readiness Plan](incident-readiness-plan-template.md) if one exists)

### 7. Q&A and wrap-up (10–15 minutes)
- Open floor for questions
- Hand out / share the one-page staff takeaway

## Facilitation notes

- Keep it conversational — ask questions, invite examples from the room, avoid a one-way lecture
- Never call out or embarrass an individual, even in jest
- If a simulated phishing test was run as part of [Email and Phishing Readiness](../03_Services/email-and-phishing-readiness.md), reference results only in aggregate, never by name

## Follow-up

- Send the one-page takeaway to all attendees within 1 business day
- Send brief, non-identifying debrief notes to the business owner/ops lead (themes observed, not individual performance)
