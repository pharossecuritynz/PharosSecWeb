# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

New Zealand small and medium business owners and operations leads (typically 15–60 staff) with no in-house security expertise. Most already use an outsourced IT provider or MSP and want independent advice they can actually act on, not a replacement for that relationship. They arrive after a trigger event (a peer's breach, a near-miss fraudulent invoice, a cyber insurance renewal, or a customer's security questionnaire) rather than proactively. They are wary of being sold to, short on time, and more receptive to clarity and prioritisation than to a long technical audit. See `02_Business_Strategy/customer-profiles.md` for the three detailed buyer profiles this covers.

## Product Purpose

The website is the front door for a founder-led, independent cyber security advisory practice. It exists to make a nervous, non-technical business owner feel understood within seconds, explain what Pharos Security does (and explicitly doesn't do) without jargon, and move them toward a no-obligation first conversation. Success is a qualified visitor booking the 15-minute Security Conversation, an Independent Security Review, or IT Provider Security Assurance, or contacting Pharos directly, having already absorbed why "independent, plain-English, right-sized for SMEs" matters before they ever get on a call. See `03_Services/service-catalogue.md` for the full v2 service architecture.

## Positioning

For NZ SME owners who need to understand and reduce cyber risk but lack in-house expertise, Pharos Security is the independent advisory that translates cyber risk into clear, practical action, never selling products, never inflating urgency, and calibrated to what a business with limited budget and no security staff can realistically do. Every screen reinforces one claim: this is a trusted advisor, not a vendor.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: "Book a 15-minute conversation" (mailto, no form/scheduler yet). Secondary fallback: direct email contact (`pharos.security.nz@gmail.com`) for visitors not ready to commit to a review.
- The line a visitor should remember after 10 seconds: "Independent, plain-English cyber security advice, sized for my business, not trying to sell me something."
- Belief ladder (in order): (1) this problem is relevant to a business my size, not just enterprises → (2) this advisor has no product to sell, so the advice is unbiased → (3) the advice will be proportionate and actionable, not a 40-point framework I can't action → (4) talking to them costs nothing and creates no pressure → (5) I should book a no-obligation conversation now.
- Proof on hand: none yet — pre-launch, no delivered engagements. Testimonials, case studies, and any partner/referral logos should be added to `.impeccable/assets/proof/` as they become available; until then the site should carry its credibility through positioning clarity and tone, not fabricated or placeholder social proof.

## Brand Personality

Calm, practical, direct, quietly confident. Sounds like an experienced advisor sitting across the table, never a vendor, technician, or scaremonger. Premium boutique consultancy feel, closer to a specialist design studio than a security vendor or SOC. Full detail in `01_Brand/tone-of-voice.md` and `01_Brand/visual-identity.md`.

## Anti-references

- Generic MSP/IT-vendor template sites: stock cybersecurity imagery (glowing padlocks, binary/matrix overlays, blue grid backgrounds), hero-metric SaaS clichés, hooded-hacker photography.
- Fear-based security marketing: breach statistics as scare tactics, countdown urgency, red/black aggressive colour schemes, weapon or crosshair iconography.
- Military/tactical styling: camo, stencil fonts, combat imagery, "hacker/SOC" aesthetics generally.
- Big-4/enterprise consultancy look: stiff, over-produced, corporate-generic — Pharos is explicitly not built for the enterprise segment and shouldn't look like it is.

## Design Principles

- **Advisor, not vendor.** Every visual and copy choice should read as independent counsel, never a sales pitch — restraint over persuasion tactics.
- **Right-sized, not enterprise-cosplay.** Calibrate polish and information density to a boutique founder-led practice; don't borrow scale signals (dense dashboards, enterprise logos, oversized stat blocks) from a segment this brand explicitly doesn't serve.
- **Calm authority over urgency.** Navy-anchored, generous whitespace, slow/subtle motion — clarity through composure, never alarm.
- **Abstract guidance, never literal threat.** The beacon/light motif represents direction, not danger; no literal security iconography (padlocks, shields, crosshairs) as primary devices.
- **Plain English at every layer.** Interface copy follows the same voice rules as written content: lead with meaning over mechanism, one idea per sentence, no unexplained acronyms.
