# Differentiation

## The core differentiation statement

Pharos Security is the only option in the market that is simultaneously: **independent** (no products to sell), **right-sized** (SME-only focus), and **calm** (no fear-based selling). Most competitors have at most one of these three.

## Differentiation matrix

| Differentiator | Pharos Security | MSPs | Enterprise consultancies | Product vendors |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Independent (no product margin) | Yes | Rarely — usually resells tools | Sometimes | No |
| Priced/scoped for SMEs | Yes | Yes (bundled) | No | Varies |
| Plain-English, non-technical delivery | Yes | Rarely a focus | Rarely | Rarely |
| No fear-based sales tactics | Yes | N/A | Varies | Often relies on fear |
| Works *with* existing IT provider | Yes, by design | N/A (they are the provider) | Rarely coordinates | Rarely coordinates |
| Dedicated advisory relationship (not just a tool) | Yes | Sometimes | Yes, at high cost | No |

## The three things to repeat consistently

1. **"We don't sell products."** This single fact does more differentiation work than anything else — it means every recommendation is trusted by default.
2. **"We only work with SMEs."** Not a downmarket compromise — a deliberate specialisation that means the advice is actually calibrated to the client's reality.
3. **"We work with your IT provider, not around them."** Removes the biggest objection (fear of being told to replace their MSP) and turns a potential competitor relationship into a referral source.

## Proof points to build over time

These aren't claims to make yet, but a roadmap for what should back up the differentiation as the business matures:

- Client testimonials specifically referencing "clarity" and "no jargon" (the brand's core promise)
- Case studies showing a client moving from "anxious and unsure" to "clear plan, implemented in 90 days"
- Referral relationships with 3–5 MSPs actively sending clients
- A visible content presence (LinkedIn, checklists) that demonstrates plain-English expertise before a sales conversation ever happens

## What NOT to compete on

- **Price alone.** Competing purely on being cheaper than enterprise consultancies is a race to the bottom and undersells the value of independent, specialised advice. Compete on *fit and clarity*, not just cost.
- **Breadth of technical capability.** Don't try to out-feature MSSPs or pentest firms. The differentiation is deliberately narrow and deep, not broad.
- **Speed/volume.** This is a boutique, relationship-driven practice — don't position around high-volume, fast-turnaround commodity service.
