# Target Market

## Primary market

New Zealand small and medium businesses (roughly **5–100 staff**) that:
- Have no in-house cyber security expertise (may have an internal IT person or team, but not a security specialist)
- Often already use an outsourced IT provider or MSP for day-to-day support
- Handle data or systems where a compromise would meaningfully hurt the business (customer data, financial systems, operational technology, professional/client trust)
- Have enough scale and margin to invest in advisory services, but not enough to justify an in-house security hire

## Why this segment is underserved

- Enterprise security consultancies price and scope for organisations with dedicated budgets and security teams — inaccessible and irrelevant to a 20-person business.
- MSPs bundle basic security as a line item but are rarely positioned (or incentivised) to give an independent assessment of *their own* environment, or to prioritise beyond "here's what we already provide."
- Generic advice online (checklists, blog posts) isn't tailored to a specific business's actual risk and doesn't translate into a concrete action plan.
- Many SME owners know cyber security matters but don't know where to start, what's actually urgent, or whether they're already "doing enough."

## Best-fit industry sectors (highest natural fit)

| Sector | Why it fits |
|---|---|
| Professional services (law, accounting, financial advisory) | Handle sensitive client data; reputational risk from a breach is high; often have some compliance awareness already |
| Property and construction | Growing digital footprint (accounting, project systems), often thin IT resourcing |
| Healthcare and allied health (small practices) | Patient data sensitivity, privacy obligations, limited IT sophistication |
| Retail and e-commerce (SME scale) | Payment systems, customer data, growing reliance on cloud tools |
| Trades and services businesses with growing back-office systems | Often have outgrown ad-hoc IT habits but haven't formalised security |
| Not-for-profits and membership organisations | Sensitive member/donor data, typically very limited security budget or awareness |

## Less-fit segments (deprioritise or refer out)

- Micro businesses (1–4 staff, minimal digital footprint) — may not have budget to justify a paid engagement; consider a lighter-touch/self-serve offer (checklists) instead.
- Large enterprises (200+ staff, dedicated IT/security function) — needs exceed what a solo advisory practice should take on; refer to a larger consultancy.
- Businesses seeking penetration testing, compliance certification audits, or active incident response — outside scope; refer out per [risk-boundaries.md](risk-boundaries.md).

## Buying triggers (why a prospect reaches out now)

- A recent scare: a phishing email nearly succeeded, a supplier or peer business was compromised, an insurer or customer asked about security posture
- Renewing cyber insurance and being asked security-readiness questions they can't answer
- Onboarding a larger customer who requires basic security assurances
- General discomfort: "we know we should be doing more about this but don't know what"
- A new leadership hire (ops manager, CFO) who wants visibility into risk they've inherited
