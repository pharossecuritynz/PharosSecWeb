# Target Market

**Status note (2026-08-22):** tightened from 5-100 to 5-50 staff under the Pharos v2 directive, and buyer/trigger lists added. See `00-business/decisions.md`.

## Primary market

New Zealand organisations with roughly **5 to 50 staff** that:

- have limited or no dedicated security staff
- use outsourced IT support or a generalist internal IT person, not a security specialist
- run on Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace and are generally cloud-heavy
- have a relatively small operational footprint
- are led by an owner or manager who understands security matters but doesn't know how to judge it
- have limited internal governance
- have security decisions currently driven mostly by their IT supplier
- face growing customer, insurance, or contractual security requirements
- have no interest in employing a security team

## Why this segment is underserved

- Enterprise security consultancies price and scope for organisations with dedicated budgets and security teams — inaccessible and irrelevant to a business this size.
- MSPs bundle basic security as a line item but are rarely positioned, or incentivised, to independently assess their own environment or prioritise beyond what they already provide. This isn't a failing on the MSP's part; nobody is well placed to mark their own homework.
- Generic advice online (checklists, blog posts) isn't tailored to a specific business's actual risk and doesn't translate into a concrete plan.
- Many owners know security matters but don't know where to start, what's genuinely urgent, or whether they're already doing enough.

## Likely buyers

Owner, managing director, general manager, COO, CFO, operations manager, practice manager, office manager, internal IT manager, board member.

## Likely trigger events

The events that turn a business from "we should probably look at this sometime" into "we need to talk to someone now":

- a suspicious email or near-miss
- invoice fraud or an account compromise
- a customer security questionnaire the business can't confidently answer
- a cyber insurance renewal asking questions they don't understand
- a major customer asking about security for the first time
- getting a new MSP, or leaving one
- being quoted for expensive security software and not knowing if it's needed
- a board member raising concern
- new privacy obligations
- business growth or an acquisition
- an exposure discovered online (a leaked credential, an exposed service)
- a near miss at a peer business
- an actual cyber incident
- simply wanting reassurance before something goes wrong

## Best-fit industry sectors (highest natural fit)

| Sector | Why it fits |
|---|---|
| Professional services (law, accounting, financial advisory) | Handle sensitive client data; reputational risk from a breach is high; often have some compliance awareness already |
| Property and construction | Growing digital footprint (accounting, project systems), often thin IT resourcing |
| Healthcare and allied health (small practices) | Patient data sensitivity, privacy obligations, limited IT sophistication |
| Retail and e-commerce (SME scale) | Payment systems, customer data, growing reliance on cloud tools |
| Trades and services businesses with growing back-office systems | Often have outgrown ad-hoc IT habits but haven't formalised security |
| Not-for-profits and membership organisations | Sensitive member/donor data, typically very limited security budget or awareness |

## Less-fit segments (deprioritise or refer out)

- Micro businesses (1 to 4 staff, minimal digital footprint) — may not have budget to justify a paid engagement; the Free Security Health Check is the right offer here, not a paid review.
- Larger organisations (50+ staff, dedicated IT/security function) — needs exceed what a solo advisory practice should take on; refer to a larger consultancy.
- Businesses whose actual need is penetration testing, compliance certification audits, or active incident response — outside scope; refer out per `risk-boundaries.md`.
