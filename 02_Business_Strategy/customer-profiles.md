# Customer Profiles

Three illustrative buyer profiles to guide messaging, service design, and sales conversations. These are composites based on the target market, not real clients.

---

## Profile 1: "Cautious Caroline" — Professional services owner

- **Business:** 18-person accounting or law firm
- **Role:** Managing partner or practice manager
- **Cyber maturity:** Low-to-moderate. Uses Microsoft 365, has an external IT provider, no formal security policies.
- **Pain points:** Worried about client data exposure and professional reputational risk. Has heard about firms being hit by ransomware or business email compromise. Doesn't know what questions to ask her IT provider.
- **Trigger:** A peer firm was compromised via a fake invoice email; a client asked about her data security practices.
- **What she needs:** A clear-eyed assessment of where she stands, reassurance about what's already working, and a short list of priorities she can action or hand to her IT provider.
- **Best entry point:** Cyber Risk Review, followed by Security Policy and Checklist Starter Pack.
- **Objections:** "Is this going to be expensive?" "Will this just tell me to buy more software?" "I don't have time to manage a big project."

## Profile 2: "Practical Pete" — Trades/construction business owner

- **Business:** 35-person construction or trades company
- **Role:** Owner/operator, hands-on, not tech-focused
- **Cyber maturity:** Low. Growing reliance on accounting software, project management tools, and email for invoicing. Staff use personal devices.
- **Pain points:** Recently had a near-miss with a fraudulent invoice payment request. Doesn't fully trust his IT contractor to flag risk proactively — feels like they just fix things when broken.
- **Trigger:** Renewing cyber insurance and being asked security questions he can't answer confidently.
- **What he needs:** Straightforward, non-technical guidance and a short, achievable list of fixes — not a lecture.
- **Best entry point:** Email and Phishing Readiness, or Incident Readiness Workshop.
- **Objections:** "I don't have time for a big workshop." "My IT guy already handles this, right?" "What's this actually going to cost me?"

## Profile 3: "Growing Grace" — Ops/finance lead at a scaling SME

- **Business:** 60-person retail, e-commerce, or services business
- **Role:** COO, Ops Manager, or CFO
- **Cyber maturity:** Moderate. Has some policies in place but knows they're ad hoc. Managing rapid headcount growth.
- **Pain points:** Onboarding new staff and systems faster than governance can keep up. A recent new enterprise customer asked for a security questionnaire she struggled to complete confidently.
- **Trigger:** A new customer contract requires basic security assurances; internal awareness that "we've outgrown our current setup."
- **What she needs:** A structured uplift plan she can present to leadership, plus staff awareness training to close the human-risk gap as the team grows.
- **Best entry point:** SME Security Uplift Plan, Staff Cyber Awareness Sessions, and Ongoing Cyber Advisor Support as a longer-term relationship.
- **Objections:** "How is this different from what our IT provider already does?" "Can you work with our existing systems and vendors?" "We need this to scale with us, not be a one-off."

---

## Common threads across all profiles

- None of them think of themselves as "at the point of needing a security consultant" until a trigger event happens
- All are more receptive to *clarity and prioritisation* than to a long technical audit
- All are wary of being sold to, or of a project spiralling in cost and time
- All want to keep or improve their relationship with their existing IT provider, not replace it
